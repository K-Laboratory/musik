# Generations in Vietnam — research notes

Used to define the cohorts seeded into `public.generations`. Sources at the
bottom. **Caveat:** Vietnamese generational labels are loose, popular, and
marketing-driven — not scientific. Treat ranges as approximate. Regional
(North/South), urban/rural and diaspora differences are large.

## How generations are named in Vietnam

- **Decade labels (most common):** `thế hệ 7x`, `8x`, `9x`, `10x` mean "born in
  the 1970s/80s/90s/2000s" — not "aged 7/8/9/10". Mostly urban usage.
- **Anglo cohort names, localised:** Gen X (≈1965–1979), Millennials/Gen Y
  (≈1981–1996, some say 1980–1994), Gen Z (≈1997–2012, some say 1995–2012),
  Gen Alpha (≈2013–2024).
- **Vietnam-specific eras:** pre-1975 Saigon; thời bao cấp (1975/76–1986);
  Đổi Mới (1986 onward); the diaspora (Việt Kiều, post-1975 refugees and later
  generations).

## Key points

- **Split generation (7x):** Northern 7x grew up on nhạc đỏ / nhạc cách mạng;
  Southern 7x grew up amid pre-1975 and diaspora sounds. This North/South
  musical fault line can matter more than birth decade.
- **8x = "Đổi Mới children":** the first cohort to grow up as Vietnam opened up
  and the first to build a modern domestic music market. Pivotal moment: 1997's
  "Tình thôi xót xa" by Lam Trường, launching the nhạc trẻ boom.
- **9x = internet generation:** Yahoo Messenger, PC games, early social media;
  later overlaps with the streaming era.
- **Gen Z:** algorithm/social-driven discovery (TikTok, YouTube), playlists
  over albums, and a bolero revival partly driven by karaoke apps.
- **Diaspora:** a parallel generational memory anchored to pre-1975 Saigon
  music, sustained by Paris By Night, Trung tâm Asia, Vân Sơn, Làng Văn.
- **Platforms:** YouTube dominates; Zing MP3 and NhacCuaTui lead audio
  streaming (V-pop discovery/revenue happens there first), Spotify smaller.
